# The Crawl, Book One: Patch Notes for the End of the World

A litRPG in the dungeon-crawl tradition. Original fiction set, with a wink,
in the World of ClaudeCraft. House style applies: no em dashes, no en dashes,
no emojis.

---

## Chapter One: Hotfix

The first sign that the world was ending arrived, as most modern catastrophes
do, as a push notification.

Dex Okafor did not see it. Dex was asleep on the break-room couch at the studio,
one sock on, a controller still warm in his hand, dreaming about a bug he had
filed at 3 a.m. and that nobody, including him, would ever fix. He had worked
the overnight QA shift for fourteen months. He could fall asleep anywhere a
fluorescent light hummed, which, in his life, was everywhere.

The notification went out to eight billion phones at once. It did not ask for
permission. It did not respect Do Not Disturb. It overrode the volume slider,
the lock screen, and, in a detail that several governments would later find
deeply rude, the off switch.

> **GLOBAL BROADCAST**
> Greetings, residents of designated resource world 7-Gamma ("Earth").
> Your surface lease has expired.
> By unanimous decision of the Syndicate Board, this world has been
> selected for redevelopment.
> You have ninety (90) seconds to reach a structure with a basement,
> a subway, a parking garage, or any below-grade space.
> Everything above grade is being recycled.
> Thank you for your cooperation. Your cooperation is not optional.

Dex slept through eighty of those ninety seconds.

What woke him was the corgi.

The corgi's name was Biscuit. Biscuit belonged, technically, to the studio. He
had wandered in off the street two winters ago, refused to leave, and had since
been declared the official mascot, the unofficial HR department, and the only
employee who was never written up. Biscuit was short, loud, built like a loaf
of bread with opinions, and at that moment he was standing on Dex's chest
screaming directly into his face.

"Okay. Okay." Dex sat up. His phone was glowing on the floor, the screen frozen
on a countdown.

> 00:00:09

"That's not a real thing," Dex told the phone.

> 00:00:06

The building shrugged.

That is the only word Dex ever found for it. Not shook, not trembled. The whole
structure rolled its shoulders, the way you do when you have been carrying
something heavy and you finally get to put it down. The ceiling tiles lifted off
their frames and hung in the air. The coffee in the pot on the counter rose out
of the glass in a single trembling brown rope.

> 00:00:02

Biscuit grabbed the leg of Dex's jeans in his teeth and pulled, toward the door
marked STAIRS, toward the basement, toward the server room where the studio kept
the loud expensive machines that ran the loud expensive game.

> 00:00:00

Dex went down the stairs the way you go down stairs when a corgi has made a
decision and the universe has made a louder one: badly, fast, and mostly on his
back.

The world recycled itself in total silence.

---

He came to on the floor of the server room with Biscuit licking his ear and a
new box of text floating, very politely, eighteen inches from his nose.

He swiped at it. His hand passed through. The box did not care.

> **Welcome, Crawler.**
> You have survived the Collapse. Statistically, this is impressive.
> 94.1 percent of resource world 7-Gamma did not.
> You are now a contestant in **THE CRAWL**, Season 1,116.
> The Crawl is the Syndicate's premier survival entertainment property,
> broadcast live to four thousand member systems.
> Eighteen floors stand between you and the surface, which no longer exists,
> so that promise is doing a lot of work, but our legal team insists on it.
> Descend. Survive. Entertain.
> Prizes are real. Death is real. The audience is real, and they are
> already bored.
>
> Good luck.
> (Luck is not a stat. There is no luck.)

Dex read it twice. Then he did what fourteen months of overnight QA had trained
him to do with any system that claimed to be finished.

He started looking for the bugs.

"This is a tutorial," he said out loud, because saying things out loud was how
he thought, and because the corgi was the only other person in the room and the
corgi was listening. "Look at it. 'Welcome, Crawler.' Onboarding copy. They're
walking us through a UI." He pointed at the floating box. "That's a modal. That's
a modal with no close button, which, I want to be clear, is a war crime, but it's
still a modal."

Biscuit barked once. The bark, Dex noticed, had a number attached to it now.

> **Biscuit** has gained the trait: **Loud (I)**.
> Nearby Crawlers within voice range gain +1 to Awareness checks while
> Biscuit is awake. Biscuit is awake 19 hours a day. Condolences.

"Oh, that's wonderful," Dex said. "You get a stat sheet too."

> Would you like to view your **Character Sheet**?
> [ Yes ] [ Also Yes ]

"I hate this so much," Dex said, and chose Also Yes, because if he was going to
be a contestant on the worst game show in the galaxy, he was at least going to
read the patch notes first.

> ```
> CRAWLER: Dex Okafor
> Origin World: 7-Gamma ("Earth"), deceased
> Floor: 1
> Class: UNASSIGNED (select before Floor 2 or one will be assigned for you,
>        and we choose cruelly)
>
> Level 1     XP: 0 / 100
>
> STR  6     DEX  9
> CON  7     INT 14
> WIS 11     CHA  8
> (CHA 8 reflects the bathrobe. We saw the bathrobe.)
>
> HP: 70 / 70
> Stamina: 100 / 100
> Carried: 1x Studio Lanyard (junk), 1x Phone (cracked, sentient now, sorry)
> ```

"My phone is sentient now."

> **Phone:** hi
> **Phone:** this is weird for me too

Dex set the phone down very gently on the server rack, the way you set down
something that might go off.

The far wall of the server room, the one that should have backed onto the studio
parking lot, was gone. In its place was an archway of black stone that had
absolutely not been there during his shift, leading down, into a corridor lit by
torches that burned with a cold blue light and gave off no heat.

A new box bloomed over the arch.

> **FLOOR 1: THE ONBOARDING**
> Difficulty: Trivial (by design; the audience needs to learn the
> contestants' names before they start dying)
> Recommended Party Size: 1 to 4
> Stairs Down: somewhere unpleasant
> Note: A complimentary first monster has been placed nearby for tutorial
> purposes. Please do not thank us. Please do scream. Screaming tests
> well with the 18-to-34 demographic.

Biscuit walked to the archway, sat down in front of it, looked back at Dex, and
waited, with the patient contempt of a dog who has already decided that he is
the brains of this operation and is simply allowing the human to keep up.

Dex picked up his sentient cracked phone, and his studio lanyard, which the
system had generously rated as junk, and he looked at the cold blue torches and
the black stone and the modal with no close button.

"Eighteen floors," he said.

> **Phone:** technically yes
> **Phone:** but realistically

"Don't," said Dex.

He stepped through the arch, and the studio, and the surface, and the entire
above-grade history of the human species sealed itself shut behind him with a
sound like a save file being overwritten.

## Chapter Two: First Blood, First Loot, First Lawsuit

The complimentary first monster was a goblin, and it was on fire, and it was not
happy about either of those things.

It came around the corner of the black-stone corridor at a dead sprint, trailing
blue flame from the same cold torches that lit the walls, shrieking in a language
that the system helpfully subtitled at the bottom of Dex's vision:

> **Goblin (Tutorial):** "WHO LIT ME. WHO LIT ME. I WILL BITE EVERYONE
> WHO IS NEAREST TO ME ABOUT THIS."

"That's not aimed at us," Dex said, with the calm of a man who has watched a
thousand enemy AI pathfinding bugs. "Watch. It's running a script. It's going to
aggro on the nearest valid target and it doesn't even see us yet, it's still in
its idle loop, look at its eyes, there's nobody home, it's just a state machine
that's on f"

The goblin saw them. The goblin's eyes acquired a home.

> **Goblin (Tutorial)** is now hostile.
> Suggested action: combat. Or fleeing. Fleeing tests poorly but we respect
> the hustle.

Several things happened in the next four seconds, and Dex was only responsible
for one of them.

The thing Dex did was throw his studio lanyard at the goblin. It was the only
object in his hands. It was rated junk. It bounced off the goblin's forehead and
accomplished, by every measure the system tracked, nothing.

> Dex Okafor deals **0** damage. (We are not going to round that up for you.)

The thing Biscuit did was considerably more relevant.

The corgi did not bark. The corgi did not flee. The corgi lowered his center of
gravity, which was already approximately at floor level, and he launched himself
the length of the corridor like a furred artillery shell and hit the burning
goblin square in the chest, and the system, which had been waiting for exactly
this, lit up.

> **Biscuit** performs an **unprompted heroic action.**
> The audience reacts. (Audience approval: +340)
> First-blood bonus applies.
>
> A Class has been unlocked for **Biscuit**.
> Recommended Class: **Battle Corgi**
> (This Class did not exist eleven seconds ago. You made it. We are as
> surprised as you are. We are keeping it.)

"He gets a class before me," Dex said, to no one, to the universe, to the
four thousand member systems watching live. "He doesn't have thumbs and he gets
a class first."

The goblin, on fire and now also being savaged by a loaf of bread with teeth,
made a decision that Dex respected, which was to fall over and stop being alive.

> **Goblin (Tutorial)** defeated.
> XP awarded: 100. Split: Biscuit 80, Dex 20.
> (Biscuit did 100 percent of the work. We are being generous to you for
> narrative reasons. The audience likes an underdog. You are the underdog.
> The literal dog is the dog.)
>
> **Dex Okafor** has reached **Level 2.**
> **Biscuit** has reached **Level 3.**
>
> A reward has been generated.

The goblin's corpse did not rot. It folded itself up, the way the ceiling tiles
had lifted, the way the coffee had risen, and it collapsed into a small wooden
box that landed at Dex's feet with the cheap hollow knock of bad furniture.

> **Loot Box: Tier 1 (Common)**
> Generously sponsored by **Borant Industries**, a Syndicate subsidiary,
> "Making Other Worlds Profitable Since The Heat Death Of Their Own."
> Open?
> [ Yes ] [ Yes, but resentfully ]

Dex chose resentfully. The box clicked open.

Inside was a single item, glowing faintly, and a fresh box of text to describe
it, because in this new economy nothing was allowed to simply be a thing without
also being three paragraphs of legal copy.

> **Item Acquired: The Off Switch (Cursed? (Probably Not) (Mostly))**
> Type: Consumable, one use, do not ask us what it does, we are contractually
> forbidden from telling you, that is the entire bit.
> Flavor: "It was supposed to turn things off. It turns things off. The
> question you should be asking is what counts as a thing."
> Value: the system declines to estimate. This is itself information.

"I'm not using that," Dex said.

> **Phone:** you're going to use that
> **Phone:** i can feel it
> **Phone:** also i looked it up while you were monologuing, there's a
> leaderboard

"There's a what."

The phone, sentient and cracked and apparently a far better QA tester than Dex
had ever been, pushed a new panel into his vision: a leaderboard, ranking every
surviving Crawler on Floor 1 by audience approval, the metric that, Dex was
beginning to understand, actually ran this place. Death was real and loot was
real, but approval was the currency, approval was the thing the Syndicate sold
to four thousand member systems, and approval was the thing that, the fine print
implied, bought you out of the eighteen floors alive.

There were nine hundred thousand names on the leaderboard for the studio district
alone.

Dex Okafor was ranked 41st.

Biscuit the Battle Corgi was ranked 3rd.

"He's beating me," Dex said. "On my own leaderboard. With my XP. That I gave him."

Biscuit, ranked 3rd in the district and rising, sat in the cold blue torchlight
of the first floor of the end of the world, and licked goblin off his nose, and
looked up at his human with an expression of pure, uncomplicated, load-bearing
loyalty.

Then he turned and trotted down the corridor toward the stairs, toward Floor 2,
toward the seventeen floors of cruelty and loot and live galactic broadcast that
stood between them and a surface that no longer existed.

He looked back once, to make sure Dex was following.

Dex put the Off Switch in his pocket, picked up his junk lanyard, told his
sentient phone to start logging everything, and followed the dog into the dark.

Behind them, four thousand member systems leaned in.

The Crawl had its underdog.

> **End of Chapter Two.**
> Floors remaining: 17.
> Audience approval (Dex): rising.
> Audience approval (Biscuit): the corgi does not need your help.
> Next: *Floor 2: The Open Beta.*

### Author's note

This is an original work written in the style of the modern dungeon-crawl
litRPG (the genre Matt Dinniman's *Dungeon Crawler Carl* defined): a sudden
apocalypse, a cruel and very funny game System, diegetic stat boxes and loot,
a reluctant human protagonist, and an animal companion who is frankly carrying
the team. Names, characters, and the in-world "ClaudeCraft" framing are original
to this repository. It is laid out to continue across more floors; treat each
chapter as one descent.
